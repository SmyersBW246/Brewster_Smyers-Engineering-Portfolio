# About Brewster Smyers

I’m Brewster Smyers. I study Mechanical Engineering at Bucknell, and I’ve always liked making things I can use. There’s also a lot of my life that happens away from a screen.

## Between the coast and the mountains.

I’m from Marblehead, Massachusetts. Nantucket and Sugarbush, Vermont, have also been important parts of growing up: time on boats, winters on skis, and plenty of reasons to get outside. The coast and the mountains are still the places I come back to.

I’ve always loved building things and taking them apart to figure out how they work. That interest sits alongside skiing, mountain biking and being on the water. Not every afternoon outside turns into an engineering project, but making something I can actually use has always appealed to me.

## Sometimes you start the team.

At St. Paul’s School in Concord, New Hampshire, I raced alpine, including at Pats Peak, and rowed crew. I also founded the Ultimate Frisbee team. Starting a team gave me a different way to be involved in school: creating something for other people to join.

I served as both a Grade Representative and a Dorm Representative. Sports and those roles were different parts of the same school life, with responsibilities to the people around me. Ultimate has stayed with me beyond St. Paul’s; I’m now a member of Bucknell’s team.

## About 200 miles, on foot.

Through Camp Kieve’s summer program, I completed an approximately 200-mile hike along the Appalachian Trail. It remains one of the outdoor experiences that stands out most from growing up.

Hiking and camping are still part of what I enjoy, along with skiing, mountain biking and boating. I like having a reason to spend a day outside with other people. I also follow the New England Patriots, which gives me another way to spend a Sunday, especially when the weather turns.

## Engineering, and everything around it.

I’m now a junior studying Mechanical Engineering at Bucknell University, with minors in Mathematics and Business Analytics. My interests include automotive engineering, aerospace, mechanical design and manufacturing. Bucknell Baja and the American Society of Mechanical Engineers are also part of my involvement here.

In Sigma Phi Epsilon, my Infrastructure and Social Chair work has included building risers, seating and stages, as well as organizing events. I’ve supported students through LEAP for Education and worked in hospitality in Marblehead and Nantucket. Those are meaningful parts of my experience alongside engineering.

Movember is the cause I’ve put the most into outside of class. It raises money and awareness for men’s health, especially mental health and suicide prevention. On November 29, 2026, I’m doing a 70.3-mile triathlon indoors at Bucknell’s Fitness Center: a 1.2-mile swim, a 56-mile bike and a 13.1-mile run.

I set it up so anyone can join, at Sprint, Olympic, 70.3 or full 140.6 distance, on their own or as a relay team. My fundraising goal is $5,000, and if we reach it, I’ll complete a full 140.6-mile triathlon. I’ve raised more than $800 so far. Planning it has felt a lot like an engineering project: lining up the venue, the schedule, timing, volunteers and safety, then getting people to show up.

## A model is only part of the work.

The Outdoor Landmark Chair is a good example of the work I like. I designed it in SolidWorks, selected the lumber, and cut, assembled and finished it independently in two days on a roughly $1,000 budget. It seats four to five people and is still in fraternity use.

Other projects ask different questions. Aircraft modeling combines MATLAB analysis with geometry in SolidWorks. My underwater-vehicle project begins with hull shape, displacement and buoyancy. Casting a bronze piece and turning a quarter into a ring bring the focus back to materials and manufacturing.

I’m learning to distinguish what a model predicts from what a physical test demonstrates. The project pages show what I did, the methods I used and where the work currently stands.

## What comes next

For Summer 2027, I’m looking for hands-on engineering work in design, testing, analysis or product development. I want to keep learning, contribute to a team, and get closer to the things we’re designing.
