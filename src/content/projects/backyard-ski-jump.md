## Build and iteration

When I told my parents what I wanted to build, they figured it would take me a week and a half to two weeks to finish. I had it built in about 6 hours.

I assembled the timber support frame, the slatted in-run and the rail on my own. The in-run is covered in modular bristle panels so skis slide on it without snow, and the rail sits on short timber legs with wide feet so it stays level on grass. Then I kept improving it through use, the same way I'd approach an engineering design now.

The construction photographs show the frame and finished feature, and a video still records it in use. The project predates my formal engineering education and does not include instrumented testing or structural analysis.

## How I improved it

I didn't get it right the first time. We skied it, I watched what went wrong, and I changed the design. Three changes made the biggest difference.

- **Added a ramp onto the rail.** At first you had to hop up onto the rail. A short ramp leading up to it let you ride straight on, which was easier and let you carry more speed across.
- **Adjusted the angle.** Changing the pitch of the rail kept you moving across it instead of slowing down partway.
- **Changed the rail material.** The first rail surface grabbed the ski bases. Switching to a slicker material cut the friction, so you slid across more smoothly and the skis took less wear.

## What it taught me

The project introduced a process I still enjoy: build something, observe what happens and make a practical improvement. It also gave me experience planning a small construction project and translating a rough concept into assembled parts.
