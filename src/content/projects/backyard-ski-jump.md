## Problem

My brother and I wanted to practice skiing without snow. In eighth grade, I decided to build a ski feature in the backyard. When I told my parents, they figured it would take me a week and a half to two weeks.

## My role

I built it on my own: the timber support frame, the slatted in-run and the rail. This was years before any engineering coursework.

## What I built

I had it built in about 6 hours. The in-run is covered in modular bristle panels so skis slide on it without snow. The rail sits on short timber legs with wide feet so it stays level on grass.

I didn't get it right the first time. We skied it, I watched what went wrong, and I changed the design. Three changes made the biggest difference.

- **Added a ramp onto the rail.** At first you had to hop up onto the rail. A short ramp let you ride straight on, which was easier and let you carry more speed across.
- **Adjusted the angle.** Changing the pitch of the rail kept you moving across it instead of slowing down partway.
- **Changed the rail material.** The first rail surface grabbed the ski bases. A slicker material cut the friction, so you slid across more smoothly and the skis took less wear.

## Evidence

Two construction photographs show the frame and the finished ramp, and a video still shows the feature in use in March 2020. There was no instrumented testing or structural analysis. The test was skiing it.

## Status

Completed in 2020. It is here because it is the first time I ran the loop I still use: build it, watch what happens, fix it.
